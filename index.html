A letter of apology

I am sorry for the way I behaved in your class.

Respected Ma'am / Sir, I am writing this without excuses attached to it, because I think you have heard enough of those and because none of mine would survive being said out loud. I disturbed your class. I know exactly which moment I am talking about, and so do you.

What bothers me most is not that I was caught. It is that a class is a fragile thing — you spend the first few minutes building attention in a room of thirty people, and it takes one person about four seconds to break it. I was that person. The time you lost restoring order was time you had prepared for us, and I took it from the whole room, not only from you.

I am not going to promise that I will magically become a different student. That promise is easy to make and impossible to check. So instead, here is what I will actually do, and you are welcome to hold me to it:

  • I will sit where you can see me, not where I can hide.
  • If I have something to say, it goes up as a hand, not sideways as a comment.
  • If I disturb the class again, I will not wait to be called out — I will come to you first.
  • The topic I talked through, I will cover on my own time and show you the work.

You did not owe me patience, and you gave it anyway. The least I can do is stop making it necessary. Thank you for correcting me instead of writing me off — I understand the difference between the two, and I know which one I received.

Prashant

Registration No. 12618146  ·  With genuine regret and full intention to do better
